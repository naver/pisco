## Lessons (what I would tell the next campaign)
- **Retraining the control on the new corpus was right.** The 256-token MS MARCO control landed within 2 pt of the SmolLM2 reference on every set (unpaired, so only a sanity check), so the arm-vs-control difference was one variable: chunk length.
- **Answer.** Long compressor chunks in stage 1 do not help. At 1,024 tokens the arm lost on long text (−1.1 / −3.3 pt) and on document pages (−6.2 / −4.3 pt), photos flat (paired judge accuracy, 1,500 questions per set, rate 8 tokens per slot, seeds 42 / 43). The 2,048-token arm was stopped by the owner at 68% of stage 1 and never evaluated: no claim about 2,048.
- **A dose arm should wait for the lower dose's gate.** Running stage 1 one after another (256 → 1,024 → 2,048) meant the 2,048 arm had already burnt ≈ 231 GPU-h when the 1,024 arm closed. Next time either chain the higher dose on the lower dose's gate, or write in the law that the loop may stop a dose arm once the lower dose closes (here it took an owner question and 14 h).
- **Held-out loss of stage 2 and of the finetunes foretold the loss** (1,024 arm higher at every eval point, both seeds; 64 held-out rows, never a gate). Worth a law line next time: a cheap early-warning, reported next to the judge.
- **Step-100 previews (300 questions) can point the wrong way**: seed 43 long text was +2.7 pt at step 100 and −3.3 pt at the end. Previews prove the code ran, nothing else.
- **Operational traps, now in known_issues.md:** gpu-be preemptions re-run the whole eval (judge-only resume handed off); launchers read data paths from env vars the manifest does not keep (read the reference's `training_config.yaml`); `save_total_limit: 2` deletes `checkpoint-100` by step 300 (copy it first).
